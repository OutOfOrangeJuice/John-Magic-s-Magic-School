## Transcript for History of Magic 101 Lecture 1

Alright, let's get started, then. How is everybody doing today? Enjoying the horrid rainy weather? I see a lot of rain protection charms today – it's really pouring out there. 

Anyways, hello everybody, and welcome to History of Magic 101. My name is John Magic, and I will be teaching this course for the entire semester. In fact, in case you didn't know, this is one of the few courses I normally teach personally – despite all the work I have to do managing the school and all. 

It's not because I like to talk about myself, although it's true that I do. This course will cover a general overview of the different forms of magic and how they have interacted throughout history. As someone who has mastered most forms of magic and lived through that history, I assure you that I am the most qualified person to teach this course. 

To give a quick overview: we'll start with how magic is now in the first week. Then, trace backwards era by era until we reach a point where our knowledge is, well, purely mythological. Then, we'll bring it all back together in the end. This will be one of the drier courses you take, but there's a wealth of knowledge and bad jokes in this noggin, so don't fall asleep!

Today's lecture will give a general overview of the most well-known forms of magic and where they stand today. It's actually adapted from a talk I do at the *Pathways for Mages* event in Arcania, so if you were there for that, you'll find much of the material the same. It's always good to revise, though, so please pay attention still.

We'll start off with wizardry! I've a lot to say about wizardry, because I invented it. Among the magical disciplines, wizardry is one of the more consistent and sensible ones. You won't be manipulating the elements with your mind like a sorcerer or making furniture float like a witch. As wizards, our spells are predefined and learned through study. You don't need any particular talent in magic to be a wizard – just diligence and a willingness to learn.

Of course, wizards have only been really widespread for the past few centuries, so before then you'll find very few of us. Nowadays, my Magic School is the primary source of wizard teaching, although you'll find many independent wizards teaching apprentices around the world. There are also a few up and coming wizard schools popping up, which is great! Wizardry is a very egalitarian magic, so it's always good to have more wizards. 

I won't bore you with the exact process of how wizard spells work. You should be familiar anyways from high school and MAG101. Additionally, wizardry is often studied alongside other forms of magic since it isn't really something internal to you. You could classify it as a form of 'external' magic, I suppose. I can see that this is the path for many of you here in this hall.

Now, let's get onto sorcery. Sorcery is probably the oldest and most common form of magic. Nobody really knows when it first came about, but even now it's still going strong. Those of you who are sorcerers will doubtlessly agree with me that sorcery is nothing like wizardry. Rather than learn specific spells, sorcerers each have their own individual Summit to strive towards. Each Summit is different, and sorcerers get stronger by comprehending more of their individual Summits. 

Sorcery has always been taught widely because of how intuitive it is. However, because the exact abilities a sorcerer gets is specific to each individual, it's also *really* hard to teach. That's why most of you who are sorcerers receive personalised teaching rather than academic teaching like wizards. It's simply not possible to teach sorcery in an academic setting.

Generally, sorcery tends to be less versatile than wizardry at the lower ends due to how specific it is. One of my sorcerer friends has the ability to grow plants really quickly, but not control them at all. However, as you reach milestones in your journey to the Summit as a sorcerer, your abilities expand and become more applicable to a wide array of situations. My friend gained the ability to control how her plants grow, and then the ability to disintegrate plants through her inversion. 

But that's enough on sorcery! Those of you who are sorcerers will be aware of most of that already. Let's move on to runes!

Formations, Runes, and Rune Circuits are an emerging field of magic that has seen many great advancements in the past century. They work on the same first principles as wizardry, but take a different approach that is less intuitive to most. 

Formations are the foundational order of magic – patterns that create effects. Wizard spells and runes alike are both composed from formations – but where wizard spells are typically one-off formations, runes are often installed semi-permanently, so there are fundamental differences in philosophy between the two fields. 

Runes are formations given meaning – essentially an abstraction of specific combinations of formations. There are three largely used runic languages – the empire's Divine Script, Arcanian Runes, and the Linaean Cipher. Here in my Magic School, we teach all three languages, as well as some other variations and specialised languages for certain purposes. In fact, I do believe we're the only major institution that teaches the Linaean Cipher. Archmage Linaeus was one of my students, back in the day. Unlike the other two languages, the Cipher was made from the ground up to be as efficient as possible, although it is harder to use. That's why it's used for most industrial rune magic nowadays, while Divine Script and Arcanian Runes are used more for general inscriptions and Rune Circuits. 

Speaking of circuits, Rune Circuits are the emerging field of emerging fields – an additional layer of abstraction from runes. That is, a structure that can adjust itself through complex runic logic to perform computational operations. Circuits are new, but they're already starting to revolutionise how we inscribe orbs. They've also completely shaken up the artillery mages, since they're capable of automating all the calculations artillery mages have to do. 

So if you're interested in runes, it's a great area to study. There's lots of interesting things to discover still. I think one of my archmage ascendants is working on a self-modifying circuit that can edit its own runes to adjust to the magic of whichever environment it's placed in. 

Adjacent to runes, and another emerging field on its own, is alchemy! Every since the publication of the *Laws of Living Alchemy* about a century ago, alchemists have made a great number of advancements in a variety of magical fields. Almost all the food we eat today has likely undergone some level of alchemical treatment, or was grown alchemically. In fact, in recent times, alchemy has shown the potential for solving all the problems that arise from magically growing food, so we may be on the verge of an agricultural golden age.

But not only that, alchemists have been adding to those living laws by the dozen. It feels like every few years they discover something new that shakes up the whole discipline. Transmuting stuff into gold is long behind us, now. Here at my Magic School, we actually have a world-class alchemy department with three alchemical Archmages – Archmage Ruthers is an expert on cross-disciplinary interactions, Archmage Gill has mastered elemental reagents, and Archmage Heinrich is one of the very few who claimed the title by creating an elixir of immortality. I'd like to have Ruthers do a guest lecture for this course sometime, actually. Her work on the interactions between alchemy and witchcraft is quite extraordinary. 

Alchemy is known for interacting with other fields, after all. I know there are a number of pills or elixirs that sorcerers use to get stronger, and we wizards frequently use alchemical components in advanced spells. There's a little bit of alchemy in everything, nowadays, so it's a great field to study. 

Although alchemy is based on a very hard set of rules, witchcraft is not so consistent. Witchcraft has been around for as long as sorcery. Some call it the household magic, but a more accurate term would be 'societal magic'. Unlike wizardry or alchemy, witchcraft does not prescribe a set of rules for how it functions. Rather, the rules change based on context. Witchcraft functions totally differently based on an uncountable number of factors. 

This might sound scary to you if you're primarily a wizard, alchemist, or rune-crafter, but I can assure you that witchcraft is actually quite intuitive. It also contains a lot of pretty common and consistent magical fields that we use every day, such as contract magic. Exactly what you can do with witchcraft completely changes depending on what you are using your magic on, where you are doing it, the traditions of where you're doing it, your personal history, the history of your casting target, the moon phase, what you had for breakfast that morning, et cetera. Experienced witches just go with the flow, because usually you'll get what you need if you can make it.

Anyways, witchcraft is another one of those magical disciplines that's compatible with everything. Although we don't have many courses teaching it here, that's moreso because it's almost as difficult to teach academically as sorcery is. 

We're running out of time, so I'll quickly finish up with emotional spellcasting. Emotional spellcasting has its toes in every other field of magic, although it affects internally cast magic – that is, sorcery and witchery – more than externally cast magic like wizardry or runecraft. However, it can also be viewed as a field on its own.

Emotional spellcasting is the magic of strong emotions. It's a fickle art, and some people are better suited to it than others. I, personally, have never been able to grasp it – and I'm sure there are many of you who may be the same. However, for someone who *can* use emotional spellcasting – especially in its pure state where it's not influencing another form of magic – emotional spellcasting can be immensely powerful. Those who specialise in emotional spellcasting are said to twist the very reality around themselves, making the universe bend to their every whim. However, people who are capable of reaching this point are rare. The only one I can think of who is still alive is the leader of the Mage Guild herself.

Of course, such immense power comes with drawbacks. Although emotional spellcasting is powerful and generally gives you what you want, it also comes with the danger of twisting your emotions as well. If you use anger to cast a spell, the magic will resonate with your own mental state and make you more prone to anger as well. The key to emotional spellcasting is balance – if you can maintain control over your emotions, it can be a powerful tool. If you let your emotions control you, however, there can be serious consequences. Totally losing control can even lead to wild magic syndrome. Make no mistake – emotional spellcasting is by far the most dangerous magical discipline out there. 

Alright, we are out of time. Tomorrow, we will start going over the place that each of these forms of magic have in the current times. Have a pleasant evening, everybody!
