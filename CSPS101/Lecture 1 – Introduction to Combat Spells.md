Well, depending on the demon king situation, the teacher you're supposed to have might return halfway though. Archmage Galloway is a master on the subject – he may not have written the spells himself, but he's a lot better at explaining them than I am, so you'll be in good hands.

Don't bet on it, though. Things are probably going to get worse before they get better, so assume you've got me for the whole course. Needless to say, being taught these spells by the man who invented them is quite the advantage, too. I expect all of you to pass this course.

Well, without further ado, let's get into the first lecture. You should have been issued this course's study tomes already. In this course, we will work through the first three chapters. We'll spend this lecture and a bit of the next going over the history and purpose of combat spells, and then we'll get straight into *Magic Missile* in our practicals next week. After that we'll cover some theory in lectures again, and dive into *Elemental Blast* for you to play with over the break. We'll spend the rest of our time after that on *Magic Armour* and *Magic Shield*. We will go over both, but you will only need to learn one to pass. 

Excited? No? C'mon, this is one of the most popular courses we have. Show some enthusiasm! Every good wizard starts from somewhere.

Each of the spells we'll be going over in this course were developed by myself, on a battlefield you won't have heard of. I won't bother with the history – ya'll can take MHIST if you want to learn about my personal history. I just want to first establish what the spells you will learn were originally made for. 

So, the battlefield! I was a younger wizard then. This was my first time in a proper large scale battle, and the spells I had already figured out were pretty much useless. *Magic Missile*, *Elemental Blast*, and the basic defensive spells became the cornerstone for my survival and contribution to the battle.

Well, those and *Fireball* as well. You'll learn that one in CSPS102. Anybody want to hazard a guess on why these spells were so effective on a battlefield?

Yes, you there, in the green hat. 

Destructive power? Not quite. In fact, when I invented these spells, I was capable of much more destructive magic. That was the whole reason I got drafted to fight, actually. Your original teacher, Archmage Galloway, is in the same situation now. Any other ideas?

Go ahead!

Efficiency? That's a good answer. You're not *wrong*, but that's not quite the answer I'm looking for. Pay attention now, you lot. This'll be on the written exam, so it's free marks if you memorise it. One more guess! Anyone?

Nobody wants to guess? Alright, just this once, I'll give it to you.

The thing that made these spells so effective on the battlefield is none other than simplicity. It takes quite a lot of concentration to cast a spell. You're wizards – you can't just fling the elements around like a sorcerer or chuck a drawer at someone like a witch. All well and good in duels, where your only focus is on your opponent.

But on the open battlefield, you can't lose focus on your surroundings. Putting your whole mind into casting a powerful combat spell is just asking to get stabbed in the back, or have some nasty curse blindside you. I had to fight against the Torsion Mage during that war. Believe me – you do not want to be putting your all into a spell only to get blindsided by his signature curse. 

The spells in this course are very simple, even among other first year spells. You'll find that very much intentional. Another hint for the final exam – you will not only be tested on whether you can cast these spells, but also how quickly you can cast them. By the end of the semester, I expect you to be able to cast all three of the spells you will have learned by second nature. 

In a combat situation, being able to cast quickly and easily can make a huge difference. I continued to use and develop these spells after the war, and you will find them useful as a foundation for some of your future studies. 

So, for the rest of the lecture, allow me to give you a brief overview of the usage situations for each of this course's four spells. I want you all to start developing an understanding of what spells to choose for which situations. This skill will apply across all your courses – the advantage of being a wizard is that there isn't a limit on how many spells you can learn. You can go wild; learn magic for the most niche situations. But a wizard will always need to pick the right spell for the situation.

Let's start with *Magic Missile*. At your level, this spell will be the cornerstone of your combat repertoire. The variant I will be teaching you in this course has been developed over centuries and optimised to be a near perfect combat spell with a wide variety of use cases. If you have absolutely nothing else that will work, you can almost always fall back on *Magic Missile*.

If you have a spellcasting implement with you now, please raise it. If you haven't brought one, you should – at your level it's important to keep a wand, staff, casting orb, or other spellcasting implement with you at all times. The magic missile variant I will teach you for this course requires one, as well. It was originally intended to be cast with a wand, staff, sword, or other long object – but those of you who use orbs will find a section at the end of the chapter about other guidance methods. You won't be marked on guidance method, so long as you can hit the targets in the exam.

Now, those of you who are more familiar with casting may notice a large number of things in the spellwork that would normally be considered bad practice. We'll go over the details in some of the lectures on this spell, but the short explanation is that they make the spell easier to cast. You don't need to adjust power of cast with inbuilt safety mechanisms – it's just a *Magic Missile*, after all. If one missile isn't enough, you can always just cast another one. 

Onto *Elemental Blast*. This spell's a weird one that you might not have seen very much before. It's a simple enough spell, really. Pick an element, and shoot it out in a directed wave – preferably from your spellcasting implement, as we don't want you to be burning or freezing your hands. We teach this spell to you in this course for three primary reasons. Firstly, it's useful for creatures with specific elemental weaknesses – if you need to, I don't know, blast a lesser fire elemental with water or something. It's also one of the simplest and most open-ended spellframes you'll ever see. In fact, there are multiple entire disciplines with foundations in this spell. If you are going on to study elemental spellcasting, prepare to see this spell a lot in your higher-level spellwork.

We also teach this spell because of its volatility. It's hard to control, and there's a good reason for that. The goal of learning this spell isn't just learning elements to pick, but also learning how to use it to its fullest potential without endangering your allies.

As for *Magic Armour* and *Magic Shield*, these two spells are very similar. The only difference is shaping. So, you can pick which one you like more. Armour is more useful of you're planning on going deep into the melee or using your spells in actual combat situations – although you'll find it takes a bit more concentration to cast and maintain – whereas shield will give you a good foundation for studying higher-level barrier spells. It's also a good spell to hide behind for artillery mages. 

Either way, you'll have to be able to cast the spell quickly in a combat environment and maintain it, even while casting other spells. That's the main thing we'll be looking for – the strength of the shield or armour doesn't matter so much as how quickly and consistently you can make it. 

And with that, our time is just about up. Does anybody have questions before I close the lecture?

A demonstration? Sure, I'll give you a taste of what you'll eventually be capable of. When it comes to simple spells like these, you never truly outgrow them.
